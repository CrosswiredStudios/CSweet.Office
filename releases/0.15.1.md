# Office 0.15.1

Fixes agent runtimes that booted slowly losing their assignment lease.

From Super Awesome Games on 2026-11-13. Four runtime assignments failed with `runtime-assignment-lease-expired`
at exactly 60 seconds after assignment (02:40, 03:40, 03:45 and 04:29 UTC). Each had taken 44–52 seconds from
assignment to `Running`. All 194 assignments that booted in 37 seconds or less were unaffected. One of the four was
QA's first start of a new agent version. It took the ticket's in-flight QA attempt with it, so the ticket showed a
heartbeat failure until work recovery started a fresh attempt about seven minutes later.

- **Cause.** Headquarters grants a 60-second lease when it assigns a workload, and the Node's renewals keep it alive.
  `OfficeWorker.ExecuteAssignmentAsync` sent its first renewal only 20 seconds after reporting `Running`. Everything
  before that went unrenewed: waiting for a workload slot, fetching a new agent artifact, creating and starting the VM,
  and opening the guest channel. A boot longer than about 40 seconds therefore expired before its first renewal.
- **Fix.** Renewal runs as its own loop from the moment the Node holds the assignment until it ends, every
  `LeaseRenewalInterval` (20 seconds). A renewal that can't be sent is logged and retried on the next tick instead of
  failing the workload. Headquarters still re-checks fencing, node health and grants on every renewal, and fences on
  rejection.
- Renewal still never extends the signed authorization window, so `SEC-INV-07` and the fencing rules are unchanged.
  `docs/20-security/04-workload-authorization.md` now says when renewal starts.

Upgrade the Office the usual way (drain, wait for zero active assignments). Headquarters needs no change.

Verification: `CSweet.Office.Tests` 271 passed, 0 failed. This includes the new
`A_slow_boot_keeps_renewing_its_assignment_lease_before_the_workload_is_running`, which failed against 0.15.0 (no
renewal before `Running`). Not yet run on a real Hyper-V host.
